The sea shushed against the stone like someone asking it to be quiet.
When the tide turned, I pulled the line, and the bell spoke across the black water.
Nobody was out there to hear it.
I wrote the word "nobody".
At the dead of low water, the lamps came.
Through the shutter I saw eleven small flames walking down from the village in no hurry.
They stopped at my door.
I heard breathing, and a woman say, "Not tonight."
Then the lamps turned and went back up the hill.
The bell did not ring.
I wrote two words.
Bell silent.
At dawn I turned the key.
My ankle was raw, a red band like a bracelet.
I did not remember pulling.
The new page had no grey smear.
In forty-one years it had never once been clean.
No prankster.
No walker.
The gap was not a nightly crime.
It was one old night, and I would have to go back and stand in it.
I opened the atlas to that dawn.
Two sets of prints came in from the sea.
A woman's boots, the left heel worn.
Beside them, small and bare, a child's.
In the margin the harbour keeper had written a line about the bell.
I skipped it as weather.
At the post, the small prints went on toward the village.
The large ones stopped.
Two heels, planted deep, facing the village lamps.
I did not close my eyes.
I went out to the Drowning Stone, because stone does not talk.
