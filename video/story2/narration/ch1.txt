Fourteen thousand, nine hundred and seventy-five nights, and the ledger is one short.

I know who is missing. I counted her out myself, when I was seven, and I let her drown.

I am Maren Voss, the tide clerk of Harrow Deep.

Tonight a storm tide broke into the strongroom, and the oldest ledger floated off its shelf, smelling of lamp oil. I opened it to the night it happened, expecting one line in a child's hand. I found two pages.

Twice a day, for about six hours each time, a stone road lies open to the mainland. A bell on a post rings when the water turns. Beside it hangs a short rope, for anyone the sea has caught.

My work is two columns. A person who walks out is a stroke on the left, and that is OUT. A person who walks home is a name on the right, and that is IN. The columns must agree, and they have not agreed since I was seven.

The village calls the difference the Ghost. They say the drowned clerk walks the road at night and rings for the one who is not home. I do not believe in the Ghost. I believe in arithmetic. One went out. One did not come back.

The first page was my mother's last night. The second was dated for a night that had not happened yet.
